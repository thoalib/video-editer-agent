# Directing each beat

A repertoire, not a checklist. Every designed beat must help the viewer understand or feel something.

| What the viewer needs | Treatment | Craft requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Follow a clear statement | Clean footage or one key phrase | Natural delivery, readable English |
| Remember the hook / claim | chestCaption, speaker push on the key word | Key word big; push small (6–10 %) |
| Follow several items or steps | listCard rows, routeMap markers, pointerLines | Reveal each when it is said; hold to read |
| Compare alternatives | phone swipe, splitScreen | Same scale, units and visual weight on both sides |
| See an example / demo | chatMock or framed real footage | Content true to what is said (numbers, names) |
| Feel a payoff | reward badge, bloom, neonChart | Earned once or twice per video, synced with sound |
| Change topic | shot change with a motivated move | Brief; no transition by habit |

For each beat record in `work/notes.md`: timeline interval, the spoken trigger, the intended viewer
understanding, the treatment and why, the English copy, entrance/hold/exit, sound cue.

## Compose for the content
- Choose a focal subject and reading order; give speaker, graphic and caption their own space.
- Align related elements to shared edges/baselines; consistent gaps within a group.
- Longer copy needs a wider card or a different arrangement, not smaller text.
- During a long graphic explanation, keep the speaker present (circle, split, inset) when their delivery matters.
- Crop insets to face and upper torso; never squeeze a whole landscape frame into a small inset.
- A short speaker opening establishes who is talking. Zooms direct attention; they do not fill pauses.
- Check intermediate frames of every move: no collisions, duplicate faces or surprise jumps.
- No term needs a full-screen graphic just because it is in the transcript.
